Our approach

An integrative approach to drug discovery.

Drug discovery depends on understanding how a compound’s chemistry, three-dimensional shape, and biological environment work together. Reliable measurements and molecular models help us interpret activity, investigate selectivity, and identify questions that experiments can resolve. Our approach connects these sources of evidence to build better-supported design hypotheses.

We begin by curating chemical and biological data, examining protein structure and behavior, and exploring molecular conformations and stereochemistry. These insights guide compound design and synthesis. We characterize the resulting molecules and evaluate their biological activity, then use the experimental findings to refine our models and the next set of designs.

This iterative connection between computation and experiment helps us prioritize informative studies, recognize uncertainty, and make the reasoning behind a discovery program reproducible. Our research develops methods and chemical strategies that can be adapted to different targets and therapeutic questions.

Research focus

Data Curation

Reliable evidence begins with a defined dataset.

Our work makes the decisions behind a biomedical dataset explicit: which chemical representations, assays, endpoints, units, and source records belong together. Dataset contracts, provenance, and quality diagnostics help turn heterogeneous pharmacology records into reproducible research assets.

Our accepted Digital Discovery paper presents a reusable, OECD-anchored curation and benchmarking framework demonstrated for opioid receptors. CURATO-OPIOID makes pharmacological mechanism and assay readout explicit before modeling, with traceable curated data, a versioned ontology, and scaffold-locked external validation.

  • Dataset contracts
  • Pharmacology semantics
  • Provenance

Contact Networks

Reading protein structure through its connections.

A protein is more than a structural overlay or a binding pocket. We study how networks of residue contacts change across molecular states and related proteins, using topology-aware comparisons to locate gained and lost contacts throughout a protein domain.

Our CDK-family study examines how selective CDK5 inhibitors relate to contact redistribution near the ligand and in the distal C-lobe. These structural comparisons provide a quantitative view of selectivity and a foundation for testable medicinal-chemistry hypotheses.

  • CDK5 selectivity
  • Distal structural changes

Protein Timing

The kinetics clock: defining what a rate measures.

Our kinetics-clock research asks how to obtain reproducible molecular transition measurements and when the available evidence supports a reported rate. State definitions, transition counting, sampling resolution, and uncertainty all contribute to the measurement.

In our alanine-dipeptide benchmark, two standard estimators agree closely when given matched states, while two conventional state definitions produce a larger difference on a fast transition. The work motivates publishing state definitions alongside reference rates and preregistering analysis rules before scoring measurements.

  • Molecular kinetics
  • Reproducibility

Binding-Site Discovery

Finding binding opportunities in their biological context.

We investigate where small molecules can bind and how protein structure, conformational changes, and the local environment shape those opportunities. Structural analysis and cosolvent mapping help us explore accessible, cryptic, and allosteric sites and develop hypotheses for molecular design.

FragFlow is one example of this work. Its depth-resolved reference accounts for probe partitioning within lipid bilayers when mapping membrane proteins. Studies of the ADP/ATP carrier, C5aR1, and TREK-1 connect local enrichment to an explicit environmental baseline and reproducible analysis rules.

  • Membrane proteins
  • Cosolvent mapping
  • Cryptic and allosteric sites

Stereochemistry

Connecting three-dimensional molecular identity with recognition.

We study stereochemistry as a central part of molecular identity and drug discovery. Our work combines conformational analysis, chiroptical spectroscopy, and electronic-structure calculations to support absolute-configuration assignments and assess the evidence behind them.

Our KChiro project compares experimental electronic circular dichroism with computed conformational ensembles. Spectral agreement, Cotton-effect sign patterns, and method sensitivity help us identify supported assignments and cases that require additional evidence. The protocol remains under development.

  • Absolute configuration
  • Chiroptical spectroscopy
  • Conformational ensembles

Conformational Search

Exploring molecular flexibility with explicit uncertainty.

Flexible molecules can adopt multiple shapes, with consequences for molecular recognition and the interpretation of computed properties. We study how to build useful conformational ensembles, compare search strategies, and assess the sampling evidence behind an ensemble used in drug discovery.

Our CertCrest project combines independent replicated searches, conformer clustering, energy re-ranking, and statistical assessments of search coverage and ensemble populations. Declared energy windows, computational methods, solvent conditions, and stereochemical assumptions define the scope of each result. The workflow identifies when additional sampling is needed and records the evidence supporting its conclusions.

  • Conformer ensembles
  • Replicated sampling
  • Coverage & uncertainty
  • Energy re-ranking

Molecular Design & Synthesis

Turning molecular insight into compounds we can test.

Our team brings structure-guided design and synthetic chemistry together to develop bioactive molecules. In our CDK5 program for Alzheimer’s disease research, kinase contact-network comparisons inform hypotheses for selectivity, while modular synthesis lets us vary substituents and functional groups. We have prepared 46 small molecules across Boc-protected, free-base, imine, and amide series.

The synthetic route begins with N-alkylation or N-benzylation using alkyl or benzyl bromides and sodium hydride in DMF. Palladium-catalyzed C–N coupling with Brett-Phos Pd G3 and cesium carbonate builds the protected derivatives, followed by Boc removal and free-base preparation. We diversify amine intermediates through aldehyde condensation to form imines and HATU/DIPEA-mediated coupling with carboxylic acids to form amides.

Purification, proton and carbon NMR, and high-resolution mass spectrometry establish the chemical identity of the compounds. Ongoing CDK5/p25 testing connects these synthetic series to biological evaluation and will guide lead selection and subsequent optimization. Potency and selectivity are still being established.

  • CDK5 selectivity
  • Structure-based design
  • Analogue synthesis
  • NMR & HRMS
  • CDK5/p25 evaluation
